What your Google Business Profile does well

Your profile is how you show up in Google Maps and the local “map pack.” It holds your hours, phone number, reviews, photos and directions. It's free, and every local business should have one.

What it can't do on its own

  • Answer the questions people check before calling. Do you serve my neighborhood? Do you do this specific job? Are you licensed? A profile has room for a short description and a service list — not the full picture.
  • Rank you in regular search results. The map pack is only part of the page. Below it, BrightLocal found business websites make up 47% of Google's top 10 organic results for local searches. No website, no shot at those spots.
  • Get you cited by AI tools. When people ask ChatGPT or Google's AI for a local recommendation, 42% of the sources cited are business websites, per BrightLocal's 2026 local AI study.
  • Close the deal after reviews. Good reviews make people curious. 54% of consumers visit the business's website next — the single most common action after reading positive reviews.
  • Belong to you. Google controls your profile's layout, features and rules. A website is yours.

The counterpoint

When a profile alone might be enough.

It would be dishonest to say every business needs a website tomorrow. A profile alone can carry you if:

  • You're booked out for months from referrals and repeat customers.
  • Nearly all your customers call straight from the map listing and never research further.
  • You're not trying to grow, hire, or raise your prices.

But those situations change. Referrals slow down, competitors get websites, and more searches move into AI answers that cite websites, not listings. If you want new customers who don't already know you, the profile is half the job.
